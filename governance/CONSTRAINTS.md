# Governance Constraints

Active MUST/MUST-NOT constraints. Read with [Architecture](../docs/ARCHITECTURE.md), [Decisions](DECISIONS.md) and the relevant frozen component specification; historical devlog entries cannot override them.

This is the framework's own rule set. A project that adopts the template keeps its own rules in `project-governance/CONSTRAINTS.md`, beside this file and never in it, and records the decisions behind them in `project-governance/DECISIONS.md` with their rationale (G050); an upgrade replaces this file and never touches the project's. The project's rules add to these and never override them.

## First Duty / Historical Truth

Historical Truth: Preserve historical records accurately, including mistakes, superseded assumptions, failed approaches, and contemporary conclusions. Do not rewrite history to match current understanding. Historical records provide context but never override current authoritative documentation.

Contributors MUST preserve the contemporary record when moving historical material out of authoritative documents. Corrections or later understanding MUST be identified as later context with evidence; they MUST NOT silently replace the original claims.

## Authoritative artifact first

Before designing, modifying, replacing, or discussing concrete changes to an existing governed artifact or contract, contributors and delegated workers MUST inspect the current authoritative repository source. Inspection MUST precede proposing specific replacement structure, names, fields, operations, types, wire shapes, compatibility changes, or other source-level details. This applies across governed specifications, public interfaces and executable contracts (including WSDL/XSD, API/schema definitions and authoritative database DDL), governance/tools/reconstruction contracts, and frozen artifacts or datasets.

Memory, summaries, devlog entries, plans, conversational context and secondary documentation MAY locate the source or explain history, but MUST NOT substitute for inspecting it. Delegated workers MUST inspect the applicable source themselves and identify the inspected repository paths in their proposal or handoff; delegation summaries alone do not satisfy this requirement.

If the authoritative source cannot be inspected, conceptual/domain-level discussion MAY continue. Concrete claims about its current surface and concrete source-level redesign proposals MUST be deferred or explicitly identified as unverified, and MUST NOT be presented as repository truth. Existing authority, ownership and freeze rules remain binding (G019).

## Contracts, ownership and reproducibility

- Frozen contracts and specifications MUST outrank implementation convenience; implementations MUST adapt to them. Mark a frozen artifact with the `frozen` boundary in the [classification table](ARTIFACT-CLASSIFICATION.md); a frozen path cannot enter a checkpoint.
- Published immutable artifacts (datasets, released packages, versioned contracts) MUST NOT be mutated or reused for different content: a content change takes a new version.
- Authoritative ownership MUST NOT be duplicated merely for convenience: each fact has one owner, recorded in [Architecture](../docs/ARCHITECTURE.md).
- Generated and transient build artifacts MUST NOT be committed or used as source of truth. The Mission Control datasets are such artifacts: they are generated locally into ignored folders, never committed, and remain disposable and non-authoritative. Caches, logs and transient audit files are not engineering specifications (repository ignore conventions).

## Checkpoint authority and continuity

Under G018, workers MUST follow the [checkpoint workflow](CHECKPOINT-WORKFLOW.md). Explicit lead-developer-authorized scope permits only the delegated preparation; committing requires separate explicit approval of the full exact message and bound transaction. Tools MUST NOT infer authorization. Commit authorization MUST be a deterministic approve/reject decision bound to the exact sealed transaction: explicit host Approve / Reject controls where available, otherwise the canonical tokens `APPROVE` / `REJECT` or an [approval word](CHECKPOINT-WORKFLOW.md#approval-words) that the tool matches exactly; general natural-language interpretation MUST NOT form part of that boundary. The one exception is the [early conditional approval](CHECKPOINT-WORKFLOW.md#early-conditional-approval): a go at an eligible preview approves the exact candidate that preview names only if the sealed result is identical, every check passes, every manifest path is within the committed approval-sensitivity reach, each judged by the higher of its row at HEAD and its row in the candidate's own table (a `high` path never is) and the feature switch does not read off; everything else keeps the sealed decision. The other exception is the [carry of a committed approval](CHECKPOINT-TOOL.md#diff-bound-approval) to a moved base: the lead developer's approval of the exact diff they saw stands for the carried commit only if `diff_bound_approval` reads true on main, the merge is clean, the carried diff equals the approved diff, the message is unchanged, the selection derived on the new main equals the sealed one and every selected check passes again; everything else keeps the fresh decision. A go at an eligible preview survives a moved base the same way, under the gates of the [go carry](CHECKPOINT-TOOL.md#the-go-carry), and approves only if every check passes at the seal. An exact canonical decision MUST carry the same authority whether selected or typed; the recorded channel is provenance and MUST NOT alter authorization. A verified runtime commit may enter only the narrowly defined continuity-follow-up-pending phase; pending MUST NOT be reported as PASS or completion. Full continuity validation MUST be restored before lifecycle completion. Follow-up records remain unstaged/uncommitted unless separately authorized. Historical-record commits alone MUST NOT trigger recursive devlog entries.

## Automation portability

Repository-owned authoritative automation MUST use Python where practical, with portable argument arrays for subprocesses. External tools may still be invoked from Python. Current operator instructions SHOULD use POSIX-shell-compatible command lines suitable for Git Bash/mintty and WSL, with repository-root-relative paths where practical and Python as the authoritative automation surface; quote placeholder arguments to avoid shell redirection. Platform-specific historical/manual acceptance evidence MUST be preserved; it is not active automation to rewrite for portability.

## Validation scope

**Validation scope must match change scope.** Tests MUST be selected because changed files can affect the behavior they cover. Record-only transactions MUST use documentation/index, whitespace, checkpoint integrity and continuity checks; they MUST NOT trigger component, runtime, container or full Python discovery merely because a transaction exists. Governance changes MUST use focused governance/checkpoint-policy checks. Tooling changes MUST use focused affected tooling/regression checks. Component/runtime changes MUST use relevant component gates. Cross-cutting changes MUST combine affected gates; broader suites require affected-path/behavior justification. Unknown or unsupported paths MUST block preparation until reviewed classification and appropriate checks exist. Validation MUST match both classification and the behaviors/invariants the changed bytes can affect. Reviewed impact mappings MUST select affected groups and declared dependencies; mixed/shared changes MUST use their union. Unmapped or ambiguous impact within a reviewed classification MUST escalate to its existing broader profile; unclassified paths remain blocked. Selection MUST derive from the explicit manifest and be sealed with its policy, commands and evidence. No profile may bypass G018 exact approval, integrity or continuity.

Change classification MUST describe the changed artifact separately from its validation profile (G026): `record` for history/rationale/index/status without new obligations; `governance` for normative rules, workflow and decision criteria; `tooling` for executable automation and support configuration; `runtime` for product/component code, contracts and behavior. Multiple categories are `mixed`; checks MUST be the union of affected profiles. Automation can require component/runtime gates without becoming runtime code. Normative documents MUST NOT be classified as record-only solely because they are Markdown. Record paths MUST NOT introduce obligations under lightweight checks; review and extend classification before preparation. Conservative governance classification of all DECISIONS edits is permitted; no automatic prose interpretation or worker override lowers it.

## Licensing and provenance

Every copy of the framework's material, whole or in part, MUST keep the license terms or their URL and every `Required Notice:` line ([G052](DECISIONS.md#g052---notices-travel-with-the-framework-and-contributions-are-recorded-in-files-2026-10-20-accepted)): the root `LICENSE` with a copy of the whole template, and [NOTICE](NOTICE) with a copy of `governance/` or of any part of it, an extension copied on its own included. No contributor, tool or adopting project may remove or alter them in retained material; maintaining a project does not change the terms of what it retains from the template. The `LICENSE` text beneath its Required Notice line MUST stay the canonical PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0, byte for byte.

Third-party material MUST enter with its license and notice in the same commit, kept beside it unchanged, and with an entry in the [contribution register](CONTRIBUTION-REGISTER.md). Material whose license is unknown, or does not allow it to be distributed with the framework, MUST NOT enter.

In the template's repository, contributions are recorded in files, never in a commit trailer ([contribution process](CONTRIBUTING.md)): the frozen [contribution record](CONTRIBUTION-RECORD.md), which later work MUST NOT edit, and the append-only register, which changes only when a new contributor, AI tool, kind of account, provider agreement or third-party material enters. Existing commits MUST NOT be rewritten to add or correct provenance. Neither record, nor the approval of a commit, establishes ownership, authorship or freedom from third-party rights.

## Extensions

The core MUST NOT depend on an extension ([G042](DECISIONS.md#g042---the-core-never-depends-on-an-extension-2026-10-18-accepted)): nothing in the core may import, require or name an extension, so no tool, no test of a tool and no configuration value the core needs may fail when an extension is absent. An extension MAY use the core. Removing an extension MUST leave the core consistent, as the [extensions README](extensions/README.md) describes.

## Python interpreter policy

Repository-owned Python tooling MUST use Python 3.13 or later (G024, G043). This is a minimum, not an exact 3.13, implementation, vendor, installation-path or Codex-runtime provenance pin; compatible 3.14+ interpreters are permitted. Governed commands are written `python -B governance/tools/<tool>.py ...`, where `python` means any Python 3.13 or later; a specific interpreter is run by its path. No instruction may hard-code an agent runtime's installation path.

Every governed command MUST start with the [runtime guard](CHECKPOINT-TOOL.md#runtime-guard). The guard MUST stop with one clear message under an older Python and MUST NEVER search for, discover or download an interpreter, and no worker may repeatedly rediscover the runtime, recursively search the machine or silently install one. It MUST remove every inherited `GIT_*` variable except exactly `GIT_PAGER=cat` before any Git call and MUST report the names it removed, never their values. It MUST refuse at once, without waiting, a command that writes shared state or decides on Git state and then writes while a worktree lifecycle operation holds the lifecycle lock; the guard's one registry gives every command and subcommand the mode READ, LOCAL or GATED, and a command that is not registered is GATED.

A transaction MUST be pinned to the interpreter it began on. The fingerprint of the running interpreter (resolved path, size, SHA-256 of the file, version) is recorded at the first step of the transaction and compared at the start of every later step, and a step on a different interpreter MUST refuse before it does any work. The pin is per transaction and is not a machine registration: nothing is registered, and machine-specific executable paths MUST remain untracked.

## Decision records

Every decision and every dated extension in [Decisions](DECISIONS.md) MUST have exactly one row in the audit table of the [rationale](RATIONALE.md) and one rationale entry (G029). The entry MUST land in the transaction that makes the decision effective, or in the transaction that records it when recording comes first, never as a later reconstruction. An entry states the decision in one line; why, with each reason labeled supplied by the lead developer (the lead developer's own words in the session), source-stated or agent-reasoned; the alternatives considered, each with its outcome (chosen, rejected or deferred), who decided and why, or an explicit statement that none was considered; the tradeoffs or costs; the evidence, such as commits, records and inspected paths; and when to revisit it. A worker MUST NOT present its own reasoning as the lead developer's. The rationale stays explanatory and non-normative. This applies to records dated 2026-10-08 or later; earlier records are backfilled.

## Worktree ownership

Local coordination MUST follow the [local job procedure](LOCAL-COORDINATION.md). Ignored `.local/` coordination material MUST be recorded, transferred or explicitly disposed of before its worktree is removed; it is an exception to disposable ignored output. Job state and reported worker activity MUST remain separate. Local records MUST NOT grant execution or approval authority, substitute for authoritative repository records, or become a permanent completed-job archive.

Each active transaction/mission MUST own one Git worktree, filesystem, index and branch until integration (G025). Workers MUST NOT share mutable checkouts or staging, reuse another mission's branch, or treat main as concurrent scratch. Follow the [worktree workflow](WORKTREE-WORKFLOW.md) for exclusive ownership, guarded creation/integration/removal and explicit reconciliation. What a transaction depends on MUST be preserved: its own branch and worktree row, main, tags, replace refs, notes, unnamed ref namespaces, configuration and hooks. Unrelated branches, remotes, Codex refs, the stash and other worktrees' registrations are tolerated and audited under the [protected-set ref policy](WORKTREE-WORKFLOW.md#protected-set-ref-policy); a packet prepared earlier keeps its own policy, including the [same-tree transient ref-name exception](WORKTREE-WORKFLOW.md#same-tree-transient-ref-name-policy). Neither authorizes ref deletion by a tool. G018 exact approval and completed runtime continuity remain prerequisites for integration; textual Git compatibility MUST NOT substitute for authoritative artifact inspection and affected validation.
